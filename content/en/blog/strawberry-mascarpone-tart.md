A **strawberry mascarpone tart** is one of the easiest tarts to get right in the moment, and one of the easiest to get wrong three hours later. The cream is good, so are the strawberries, but the base has softened and the slice slumps when served. Nothing was done badly: **nothing was done to keep the wet away from the crisp.**

The first one I served at a birthday party had been assembled in the morning. By four in the afternoon the pastry bent under the knife.

## Why it goes soggy

Three sources of moisture attack the base, each at its own pace:

- **The cream**, which contains water even when it is firm.
- **Cut strawberries**, which release juice as soon as they are sliced, and even more if they have been sugared.
- **The pastry itself**, if it was not baked all the way and keeps a pale centre.

Sweet shortcrust is made of butter, sugar and flour: it soaks up water like a dunked biscuit. The job is therefore to bake it right through, then to **make it waterproof** before anything goes on it.

## The pastry, blind baked all the way

For a 24 cm ring or tin:

- 250 g flour
- 125 g cold butter
- 90 g icing sugar
- 30 g ground almonds
- 1 egg
- 1 pinch of salt

Make the pastry the day before, or at least two hours ahead, and let it rest in the fridge once lined into the tin. That rest is what stops it sliding down the sides, a problem covered in the article on [why shortcrust pastry shrinks](/en/blog/why-shortcrust-shrinks/).

**Blind bake at 165 °C, 20 minutes with baking beans, then 8 to 10 minutes without**, until the base is golden, not just pale blond. A blond base is still soft at heart, and that is what softens first.

This is the one case where my oven, which runs too hot underneath, works in my favour: the base cooks through before the edges brown. In an even oven, putting the tin on the lowest rack has the same effect.

## The step almost every recipe leaves out

**Waterproof the base.** Two methods, take your pick:

1. **Egg yolk**: straight out of the oven, brush the hot base with beaten yolk and return it for 2 minutes. The yolk cooks into a thin film that acts as a barrier.
2. **White chocolate**: melt 40 g of white chocolate and paint it over the cooled base with a brush. This is the most effective method, and the flavour pairs very well with mascarpone.

I use the second whenever the tart has to wait more than an hour. It is the same idea as blind baking for a [French custard tart](/en/blog/french-custard-tart/): a base that takes a wet filling must be protected before, never after.

## The cream, denser than whipped cream

For this tart, the cream must carry the weight of the strawberries without sagging. So it goes up a notch from the usual ratio:

| Mascarpone to cream ratio | Hold under strawberries |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | fine for an hour, sags afterwards |
| **1 to 1** | **holds all day, slices cleanly** |
| mascarpone alone | dense, curdles under the whisk |

**200 g mascarpone, 200 g very cold double cream, 50 g icing sugar, half a vanilla pod.** Loosen the mascarpone with two spoonfuls of cream, pour in the rest, and whisk at medium speed to soft peaks. The full method, and how to rescue a cream that splits, is in the guide to [mascarpone whipped cream](/en/blog/mascarpone-whipped-cream/).

## The strawberries

**500 to 600 g of strawberries**, preferably small and firm.

- **Wash them before hulling**, never after: a hulled strawberry soaks up water through the top.
- **Dry them one by one** on a tea towel. Rinsing water left on the fruit ends up in the cream.
- **Never sugar them in advance.** Sugar makes them weep within minutes, which is the whole principle of maceration.
- **Whole or halved, not sliced.** Less cut surface, less juice.

For late-season strawberries, paler and less fragrant, a thin brush of warmed redcurrant jelly adds shine and slows the cut faces drying out.

## The timeline

| When | What |
|---|---|
| The day before | pastry, lining the tin, resting in the fridge |
| On the day, morning | blind baking, waterproofing, cooling completely |
| 2 hours before | cream, filling the base, fridge |
| 30 minutes before | strawberries on, glaze |

**The tart is eaten the same day.** The next day the pastry holds up if it was protected, but the strawberries have lost their firmness.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long before serving can a strawberry mascarpone tart be assembled?**
With a waterproofed base, four to six hours in the fridge without trouble. Without protection, two hours is the maximum. The strawberries are best added in the last half hour.

**Can I use shop-bought pastry?**
Yes, an all-butter sweet shortcrust works, provided it is blind baked until properly golden and the base is protected. Shop-bought pastry is often thinner and softens faster.

**Why does my mascarpone cream run under the strawberries?**
The ratio was too light, or the cream was under-whipped. For a tart, one to one is the right ratio. A runny cream can firm up for ten minutes in the freezer, without being whipped again.

**Can the strawberries be replaced with other fruit?**
Yes, raspberries, blueberries or peaches work with the same cream. Very juicy fruit such as melon or citrus is best avoided: it soaks the cream in under an hour.

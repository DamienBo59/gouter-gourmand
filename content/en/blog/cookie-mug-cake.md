A **cookie mug cake** should taste like a warm cookie with a centre that is still gooey. Most of the time it comes out of the microwave like a small sweet sponge, or worse, like a rubber ball that hardens as it cools. The microwave is almost never to blame: the fault lies with **the whole egg**, found in most recipes, and with **twenty seconds too many of cooking**.

At home, it is the treat for the Wednesdays when nothing is ready. It took me a good fifteen mugs to stop getting a cake when what I wanted was a cookie.

## A cookie, not a cake in a mug

A classic mug cake is a cake: plenty of egg, baking powder, an airy crumb. A cookie is the opposite: butter, sugar, very little liquid, and a dense texture that stays soft in the middle. The [American cookie](/en/blog/american-cookies/) owes its soft centre precisely to that low moisture and to brown sugar.

Translated to the microwave, that gives three rules:

- **no whole egg**, only the yolk. The white sets in a few seconds and creates the springy texture mug cakes are blamed for;
- **no baking powder**, or a knife-tip at most. It puffs the batter up, which then collapses and tightens;
- **a mix of white and brown sugar**, for the caramel flavour and the softness.

## Quantities, for one mug

For a 250 to 300 ml mug, preferably wide rather than tall:

- **15 g butter**, melted in the mug itself
- 1 level tablespoon brown sugar (about 12 g)
- 1 teaspoon white sugar (about 5 g)
- **1 egg yolk**
- a few drops of vanilla
- **30 g flour**, or 3 level tablespoons
- 1 pinch of salt
- **20 g chocolate chips**, or chopped chocolate

Without brown sugar to hand, white sugar alone works, with a slightly less chewy result: that is exactly what the article on [cookies without brown sugar](/en/blog/cookies-without-brown-sugar/) covers.

## The method, in two minutes

1. **Melt the butter in the mug**, 15 to 20 seconds at 800 W. It should be liquid, not boiling.
2. **Add both sugars and stir with a fork**, then the vanilla and the yolk. Stir until smooth and glossy.
3. **Add the flour and salt**, and stir just enough that no dry flour remains, scraping the bottom of the mug well.
4. **Fold in the chips**, keeping a few back for the top.
5. **Press the batter down lightly** and smooth the surface.

The batter should look like cookie dough, thick, not like cake batter that runs. If it runs, the egg was a large one: add a teaspoon of flour.

## Cooking, to the second

**40 to 50 seconds at 800 W**, no more. At 1,000 W, allow 30 to 40 seconds. At 600 W, 60 to 70 seconds.

The right sign is not a surface that looks dry all over. **The edges should be set and the centre still slightly glossy.** It finishes cooking during a minute's rest, from the heat of the mug. Wait until it looks done in the middle and it will be dry once lukewarm.

A microwave heats through the water in the batter, from the inside, and never browns anything. That is why this mug cake stays pale, and why a few seconds too many are enough to dry it out. I go into this at more length in the comparison of [cake without an oven](/en/blog/cake-without-oven/) methods, where the microwave has very precise limits.

A narrow, tall mug gives a raw centre and dry edges: the batter is too deep to cook evenly. A wide mug, with 2 to 3 cm of batter, cooks far more evenly.

## What goes wrong, and why

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Spongy texture, like a cake | whole egg, or baking powder | yolk only, no baking powder |
| Rubbery, hardens as it cools | cooked too long | take it out while the centre still shines |
| Raw centre, dry edges | mug too tall and narrow | wide mug, 2 to 3 cm of batter |
| Greasy at the bottom of the mug | butter too hot, poorly mixed | lukewarm butter, stir the sugar in first |
| All the chips at the bottom | batter too runny | one extra teaspoon of flour |

## Variations that work

- **Egg-free**: replace the yolk with 1 tablespoon of milk. The texture is more crumbly, closer to a tender shortbread.
- **Molten centre**: push a square of chocolate into the middle of the batter before cooking.
- **Double chocolate**: replace 5 g of flour with 5 g of unsweetened cocoa, a little in the spirit of the [brownie without butter](/en/blog/brownie-without-butter/), where cocoa carries most of the flavour.
- **Peanut butter**: replace half the butter with peanut butter, and reduce the salt.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can the batter be made ahead?**
Yes, up to the day before, covered in the fridge right in the mug. Add 10 seconds of cooking, since the batter is cold.

**Why does my cookie mug cake overflow?**
There is baking powder in the recipe, or a whole egg that puffs up in the heat. With the yolk alone and no baking powder, the batter barely rises.

**Can it be baked in a regular oven?**
Yes, in an oven-safe ramekin, 10 to 12 minutes at 180 °C. You then get a real little spoon cookie, golden on top.

**Can it be eaten cold?**
It is best lukewarm, a few minutes out of the microwave. Once cold, even when well cooked, it firms up noticeably.

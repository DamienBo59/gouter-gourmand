A **lemon loaf cake** nearly always disappoints in one of two ways: it tastes only faintly of lemon, even though the juice of two fruits went in, or it is dry by the next day. Both faults share the same origin: **lemon flavour lives in the zest, not the juice**, and **a lemon loaf is finished after baking**, with a syrup.

For a long time I found this cake bland, until I understood I was throwing away the most fragrant part of the fruit.

## The flavour is in the zest

Juice brings acidity. The aroma comes from the essential oils held in the yellow skin of the lemon. A loaf made with juice alone is sharp and flat; a loaf made with zest really smells of lemon.

**Rub the zest into the sugar** with your fingertips for a minute, before anything else. The sugar turns damp and pale yellow: it has absorbed the oils from the zest, which will spread through the whole batter instead of staying in little threads.

Two precautions:

- **choose unwaxed, untreated lemons**, since it is the skin you are using;
- **take only the yellow part**, on a fine grater. The white pith just underneath is bitter.

## The ratios

For a 24 to 26 cm loaf tin:

- **3 eggs**, at room temperature
- **160 g sugar**
- **the zest of 2 lemons**
- 120 g butter, melted and cooled to lukewarm, or 125 g crème fraîche
- **40 ml lemon juice**
- 190 g flour
- 11 g baking powder
- 1 pinch of salt

**For the syrup**: the juice of one lemon (about 40 ml) and 40 g sugar.

The crème fraîche version gives a tighter, very moist crumb; the butter version a rounder flavour. Both hold up well.

## Mixing

1. **Rub the zest into the sugar**, as described above.
2. **Whisk the eggs with the lemon sugar** for two to three minutes, until the mixture pales and thickens.
3. **Add the lukewarm melted butter**, or the crème fraîche, then the lemon juice.
4. **Add the flour, baking powder and salt**, and fold with a spatula just until no flour is visible.

Lemon juice poured onto cold butter splits the batter: it turns grainy, as if curdled. With eggs at room temperature and lukewarm butter, it does not happen. And if it does, a spoonful of the flour whisked in brings it back.

As with [banana bread](/en/blog/banana-bread/), the final mix must stay short: an overworked batter gives a springy crumb riddled with tunnels.

## Baking

**170 °C, 45 to 50 minutes**, on a rack in the middle of the oven.

For the neat crack along the top, two methods: rest the batter in the fridge for an hour before baking, or draw a line of soft butter down the centre with a knife after ten minutes in the oven.

The loaf is done when **a blade pushed into the centre comes out dry** and the crack is no longer glossy. In my oven, which overheats at the bottom, I put an empty tray on the rack below: without it, the base browns long before the centre has set.

## The syrup, the step that changes everything

This is the step most recipes forget, and it is the one that keeps a lemon loaf moist for several days.

1. **Heat the lemon juice with the sugar**, just long enough to dissolve the sugar.
2. **Turn the loaf out while still hot**, and set it on a rack over a plate.
3. **Prick the top with a wooden skewer**, twenty times or so.
4. **Brush or spoon the hot syrup** over the top and sides, in several goes.

A hot loaf soaks up syrup like a sponge. Cold, it lets it run off. It is the same principle as for [madeleines](/en/blog/easy-madeleines/), which are turned out hot so they do not trap their steam.

## The glaze, on a cold loaf

Lemon glaze goes **only on a loaf that has cooled completely**, or it melts and turns see-through.

- **100 g icing sugar** and **20 to 25 ml lemon juice**, added a spoonful at a time.

The right consistency is that of thick honey: it should run slowly down the sides without covering everything. Too runny, and it soaks through the cake. With no icing sugar in the cupboard, finely blitzed caster sugar works, as I explain in [icing without icing sugar](/en/blog/icing-without-icing-sugar/).

## What goes wrong, and why

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Little lemon flavour | juice only, no zest | zest rubbed into the sugar |
| Dry loaf | no syrup, or baked too long | syrup on the hot loaf |
| Grainy, split batter | juice on cold butter | ingredients at room temperature |
| Bitterness | white pith grated in | zest the yellow part only |
| See-through glaze | poured on a warm loaf | wait until fully cool |
| Sunken centre | oven opened too early, or too much baking powder | do not open before 30 minutes |

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does a lemon loaf keep?**
Four to five days, wrapped in film once cold, at room temperature. Thanks to the syrup, it is often better on day two.

**Can lime replace lemon?**
Yes, in the same quantity. Lime has a finer zest and a sharper scent, but less juice: allow three fruits instead of two.

**Why is my lemon loaf dense?**
The batter was overmixed after the flour went in, or the eggs were not whisked long enough with the sugar. The initial whisking brings in the air; the final mix has to keep it.

**Can it be frozen?**
Yes, unglazed and well wrapped, for up to two months. Glaze it after thawing.
